Response to the VoteWatch vs Vote2Watch scorecard

We agree with the scorecard's conclusion that the two repositories should be merged, with VoteWatch as the base. We have done that. We disagree with seven of its findings. Two are material: the scorecard appears to have assessed VoteWatch before its 2022 results update, and it does not mention that Vote2Watch's 2022 baseline is wrong.

Findings we contest

1. Ward preference inference and its disclosure

Contested · material
Scorecard
Inference: VoteWatch 4.5, Vote2Watch 5.0 ("neither has genuine ward presidential preferences"). Disclosure: 9.5 vs 9.0.
Evidence
Vote2Watch's ward file carries the old county figures for 2022: Ruto at 74.2% in Mandera, 72.1% in Garissa and 10.8% in Kitui. The actual 2022 shares were 20.9%, 25.4% and 27.2% (IEBC Forms 34B; matches Wikipedia's Form 34C county table, e.g. Mandera 28,351 of 135,565 valid votes). The same value is copied to every ward in a county (0 of 47 counties vary internally), yet each row is labelled constituency_imputed. VoteWatch (d7c0602) uses actual results for all 290 constituencies. 224 of them match the official Form 34B totals read by the Kenya Data Atlas; 4 were swapped back to the right constituency and 17 rescaled to the official total. The national total is Ruto 50.45% against the declared 50.49%.
Requested
Inference: VoteWatch 7.5, Vote2Watch 3.5. Disclosure: Vote2Watch 6.5, because the label describes data the file does not contain.

2. Turnout coverage

Contested · out of date
Scorecard
VoteWatch uses Form 34B turnout for 186 of 290 constituencies (8.0 vs 6.5).
Evidence
Since d7c0602 all 290 constituencies have a 2022 turnout: official atlas reads for 186, the checked Form 34B tally for 104. Vote2Watch's turnout is flat within each county.
Requested
VoteWatch 8.5, Vote2Watch 6.0.

3. Home-county treatment

Contested · factual
Scorecard
Tie at 8.0: "both now reasonably cautious".
Evidence
Vote2Watch still multiplies home counties by 1.5 (js/app.js:207, home.has(w.county)?1.5:1). VoteWatch reduced it to 1.25 (HOME_BOOST).
Requested
Vote2Watch 6.5.

4. Luo Nyanza calibration

Contested · out of date
Scorecard
VoteWatch "still produces ~44% Team A regionally and 46–48% in Kisumu/Migori" (6.0 vs 8.0).
Evidence
At d7c0602, measured on the live site: Luo Nyanza 36.2%, Kisumu 36.5%, Migori 45.9%. Vote2Watch: Luo Nyanza 38.5%, Kisumu 42.1%, Migori 42.2%. Neither is clearly closer to the July 2026 polls (Ruto 30–40% in Nyanza).
Requested
7.5 each.

5. "Automatic" project and service-delivery shocks

Contested · overstated
Scorecard
VoteWatch "still contains automatic +1.4% style project/service-delivery vote shocks" (3.0 vs 9.0).
Evidence
The ±1.4% events exist in an event library, but they are only drawn in the opt-in Live simulation. Shocks start empty (shocks:[]) and the default result applies none. The underlying point stands: the effects are hard-coded rather than measured, and Vote2Watch's "signals only" design is better.
Requested
VoteWatch 6.0 for projects and 6.0 for rallies; Vote2Watch unchanged.

6. Coalition support transfers

Contested · gap overstated
Scorecard
VoteWatch 6.5 ("candidate loyalty × local rival strength"), Vote2Watch 8.5 ("explicit candidate × region priors").
Evidence
Both vary by candidate and region. VoteWatch derives its regional factor from the other side's fitted local strength. Vote2Watch sets about 30 numbers by hand, none of them sourced. It is more granular, not clearly more defensible.
Requested
VoteWatch 7.5, Vote2Watch 8.0.

7. Run-off transfers: two omissions

Contested · omission
Scorecard
Vote2Watch 8.5 vs 7.5; no weaknesses noted.
Evidence
  • Lost votes in one case. When every candidate is on team A or B and the protest-vote slider is on, Vote2Watch's run-off drops the protest votes. At 8 points, round two counts 14,221,398 votes against 15,355,478 in round one: 1,134,080 lost (7.4%). With third contestants present, as in the default line-up, nothing is lost. We overstated this in our first reply.
  • 2022 lean as a stand-in. When neither finalist is Ruto's side, Vote2Watch splits eliminated voters by their 2022 lean. That treats Gachagua's and Nyoro's voters as Ruto-leaning, which no longer holds.
Requested
Vote2Watch 8.0.

The merged VoteWatch (0dfa09d)

It keeps VoteWatch's 2022 constituency results, corrected county shares, 290-constituency turnout, KDA ward builder, county simulation ranges, running-mate scenarios and report. It adds Vote2Watch's register scenarios, poll weighting and sensitivity switch, transfer priors, per-contestant run-off shocks, structural range, event signals and validation gate.

We left out four things from Vote2Watch: its 2022 baseline, its flat turnout within counties, its ×1.5 home boost, and its unsourced youth coefficients.

Headline results now agree closely. The remaining differences come mostly from the 2022 baseline, as the Mandera row shows. We invite a re-score of 0dfa09d.

Still open, in either repository:

  • There is no back-test against 2022.
  • Transfer and run-off priors are judgement, not data.
  • 45 constituency results have no official read to check against.
  • The 2013 and 2017 county history figures are unverified.
  • Removals from the register since 2022 are not netted out.
